Back Office · Retail Betting · UX/UI

Oversee operations without losing the details.

I designed a back office from the ground up to review transactions, bets, and users distributed across different levels of a retail betting operation. The product needed to support both broad supervision and precise searches.

Role UX/UI Designer
Product Back Office
Scope Designed from scratch
Platform Web · Desktop
01 · Operations

A distributed network creates many questions.

Administrative users need to move across different operational levels, review activity, and identify where each record belongs.

When that relationship is unclear, investigating an issue or locating a specific bet becomes slower.

The UX work connected users, operational levels, transactions, and bets so each record could be understood from its source.

🔒
Confidentiality

Developed under confidentiality.

The platform includes information related to internal processes and retail betting operations.

Some screens, data, and specific workflows cannot be shown publicly.

02 · The Challenge

Find what matters among hundreds of records.

The interface needed to support repeated searches, movement across operational levels, and a clear distinction between data, statuses, and actions.

01

Large record volume

Transactions and bets needed to be searchable without manually reviewing rows.

02

Multiple operational levels

Users needed to understand which account, terminal, or level they were managing.

03

Repeated searches

Dates, statuses, and other criteria required predictable filters.

03 · Architecture

Three tasks, three core modules.

MODULE 01

Cash Status

Review transactions and operational activity.

MODULE 02

Betting History

Search and review bets through relevant filters.

MODULE 03

User Management

Manage users and different operational levels.

04 · System

Learn once, work across the back office.

Tables, filters, statuses, and actions follow common interaction patterns.

🔒 Interface protected by confidentiality

Some operational views cannot be shown publicly.

05 · UX/UI Decisions

Design for search and verification.

01

Operational location

Clearly identify which part of the network is being reviewed.

02

Scannable tables

Make specific values easier to locate.

03

Priority filters

Treat search as a primary administrative task.

04

Distinct states

Separate data, statuses, and actions visually.

05

Reusable patterns

Repeat common behaviors across modules.

06 · Outcome

An operation that is easier to review.

The proposal brings transactions, bets, and users into a shared administrative system.

Information becomes easier to find, relate, and verify according to the operational level.
