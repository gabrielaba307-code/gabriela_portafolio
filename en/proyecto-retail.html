Back Office · Retail Betting · UX/UI

Oversee operations without losing the details.

I designed a back office from the ground up to review transactions, bets, and users distributed across different levels of a retail betting operation. The product needed to support both broad supervision and precise searches for specific records, bets, or users.

Role UX/UI Designer
Product Back Office
Scope Designed from scratch
Platform Web · Desktop
01 · Operations

A distributed network creates many questions.

Supervision does not happen at a single point of sale. Administrative users need to move across different operational levels, review activity, and identify where each record belongs.

When that relationship is unclear, investigating an issue or locating a specific bet becomes slower and increases the risk of reviewing information from the wrong part of the network.

The UX work connected users, operational levels, transactions, and bets so every record could be understood from its source.

🔒
Confidentiality

Developed under confidentiality.

The platform includes information related to internal processes and retail betting operations.

For this reason, some screens, data, and specific workflows cannot be shown publicly. This case study focuses on the product problem and UX/UI decisions without exposing sensitive information.

02 · The Challenge

Find what matters among hundreds of records.

Data volume was only part of the problem. The interface also needed to support repeated searches, movement across different levels, and a clear distinction between data, statuses, and actions.

01

Large record volume

Transactions and bets needed to be searchable without manually reviewing rows one by one.

02

Multiple operational levels

Users needed to clearly understand which account, terminal, or level they were managing.

03

Repeated searches

Dates, statuses, and other criteria required predictable filters that could be reused across tasks.

03 · Architecture

Three tasks, three core modules.

The platform was organized around the administrative tasks that needed to be performed most frequently.

MODULE 01

Cash Status

Review transactions and operational activity associated with the selected level.

MODULE 02

Betting History

Search and review bets using filters that reduce the amount of information that needs to be analyzed.

MODULE 03

User Management

Manage users and access the different operational levels that make up the retail network.

04 · System

Learn once, work across the back office.

Tables, filters, statuses, and actions follow common interaction patterns across modules.

Once users learn how a table or filter behaves, the same logic can be reused when reviewing bets, transactions, or users.

LOCATION

Know where you are operating

The selected operational level is identified before its records are displayed.

SEARCH

Find before reviewing

Search and filtering reduce the amount of data that needs to be inspected.

ACTION

Separate information from actions

Statuses and controls use different visual treatments to reduce ambiguity.

🔒 Interface protected by confidentiality

Some operational views cannot be shown publicly.

05 · UX/UI Decisions

Design for search and verification.

The main decisions support administrative tasks: finding a record, verifying its source, and acting on it with as little ambiguity as possible.

01

Operational location

Clearly indicate which part of the network is being reviewed before displaying its data.

02

Scannable tables

Column structure and alignment make specific values easier to locate without reading every row in full.

03

Priority filters

Search is treated as a primary task because many operations begin by finding one specific record.

04

Distinct states

Data, statuses, and actions use different visual levels to reduce misinterpretation.

05

Reusable patterns

Common behaviors are repeated across modules to reduce the learning effort.

06

Scalable foundation

Components can support additional states and functionality without rebuilding each screen.

06 · Outcome

An operation that is easier to review.

The proposal brings transactions, bets, and users into a shared administrative system.

Instead of simplifying by removing data, the design makes information easier to find, relate, and verify according to the operational level being supervised.

01 Transactions, bets, and users within one system.
02 Navigation designed for multiple operational levels.
03 Reusable filters and interaction patterns.
